# Held-out esterase specificity challenges

The three proteins in [specificity_challenges.tsv](../references/specificity_challenges.tsv) are characterized cellular enzymes selected to challenge transfer of a phage Lysin B label across the alpha/beta hydrolase superfamily. Selection uses published structures and biochemical descriptions, independently of the study's sequence-search outcomes. These proteins are held-out queries and are excluded from the functional reference panel. They provide a small, deliberately difficult specificity check alongside the unrelated structural-protein controls.

The exact assets are deposited PDB polymer-entity sequences. The entry, entity, author chain, equivalent chains, sequence URL and expected length are explicit. Equivalent copies within one crystal count once. Coverage calculations use the selected deposited sequence; secreted mature chains can differ in length from a UniProt precursor. Retrieval must record the raw-file checksum, normalized protein checksum, returned FASTA header and observed length before scoring. This document records curation and the intended interpretation; it reports no completed challenge search.

## Source identity and functional evidence

| Challenge | Exact structural sequence | Characterized function and selection basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cryptococcus CLE | 2CZQ entity 1, chain A; equivalent chain B; 205 residues | Hydrolysis measurements and mutagenesis accompany the cutinase-like enzyme structure. It is the closest Dali structural relative reported in the original D29 LysB study. |
| Fungal AXE II | 1BS9 entity 1, chain A; 207 residues | Acetylxylan esterase from the fungus published as Penicillium purpurogenum, currently listed as Talaromyces purpureogenus. It is another close structural relative reported for D29 LysB. |
| Pseudomonas carboxylesterase | 1AUO entity 1, chain A; equivalent chain B; 218 residues | A defined bacterial carboxylesterase with structural and substrate-specificity evidence adds an independently identified serine esterase. |

For CLE, [Kodama and colleagues (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1002/prot.22484) describe enzymatic measurements on substrates with different chain lengths and relate activity to the structure. [RCSB entry 2CZQ](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/2CZQ) identifies one unmutated protein entity, represented by chains A and B, with 205 residues. Its [official FASTA response](https://www.rcsb.org/fasta/entry/2CZQ/display) identifies the sequence as `2CZQ_1`.

For AXE II, [Ghosh and colleagues (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0907444999000244) link the enzyme to acetylated xylan substrates and the deposited structure. The [1BS9 entry](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1BS9) specifies entity 1, chain A, 207 residues and no mutations. The native 1BS9 sequence is selected; the related iodinated structure 2AXE is a different asset. Earlier [purification and activity work](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8756392/) distinguishes AXE II from the separately encoded AXE I. The [official sequence URL](https://www.rcsb.org/fasta/entry/1BS9/display) must resolve entity `1BS9_1` and pass the declared length check at execution.

[Kim and colleagues (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0969-2126(97)00306-7) characterize the structural basis of the Pseudomonas carboxylesterase's substrate specificity. [RCSB entry 1AUO](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1AUO) and its [official FASTA response](https://www.rcsb.org/fasta/entry/1AUO/display) identify entity `1AUO_1`, represented by identical chains A and B, with 218 residues and no reported mutations. The enzyme's exact structural identity is established independently. No accession is inferred for the commercially supplied Pseudomonas fluorescens lipase discussed in the D29 study, and that commercial preparation is not equated with 1AUO.

[Payne and colleagues (2009), Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2958.2009.06775.x), report Dali Z scores of 20.3 for 2CZQ and 18.9 for 1BS9, with 21% sequence identity in each structural alignment. These are structural relatives with low sequence identity. A shared fold or catalytic motif alone cannot establish the same substrate specificity. Their inclusion tests a defined cross-family assignment risk rather than broad representativeness of the cellular esterase universe.

## Fixed challenge analysis and interpretation

The execution should search these three held-out sequences against the frozen expanded reference panel using the same MMseqs search settings and the same final identity, query-coverage, target-coverage and E-value rules as the phage proteins. The declared final limits remain identity at least 0.40, both coverages at least 0.70 and E-value at most 1e-6. The reference evidence tiers retain their original caps. Challenge proteins supply no new functional seeds, neighborhood information or architecture labels. No threshold is selected from their outcomes.

The result table should retain every reported alignment, the reference identifier and evidence tier, all alignment criteria, the resulting confidence and an explicit no-reported-hit category where applicable. The principal diagnostic is the number of the three challenge proteins that receive a high- or moderate-confidence Lysin B assignment. An accepted call raises a specificity concern for the operational classifier and requires inspection of the matched sequence and reference provenance. Rejection indicates discrimination for that particular query under the frozen criteria. A sequence omitted by the search prefilter supports only the narrower statement that no qualifying call was reported under that search procedure.

The cited studies establish cellular esterase or cutinase functions. They do not establish these proteins' inactivity on mycolylarabinogalactan-peptidoglycan, and the curation does not assert that any secondary activity has been excluded experimentally. Results therefore measure potential cross-family labeling, with biochemical mycolate-substrate specificity unresolved. Three deliberately selected proteins, including structurally related enzymes, cannot estimate a general false-discovery rate or independently validate phage lysis biology. Every outcome should remain visible together with the held-out positive-control results and leave-reference-genome-out sensitivity.
