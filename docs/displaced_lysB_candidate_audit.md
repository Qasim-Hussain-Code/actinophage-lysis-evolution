# Literature-proposed displaced LysB candidates

A primary genome study proposed LysB-like proteins in three *Rhodococcus equi* phages. In ReqiPepy6 (ADD80924.1) and ReqiPoco6 (ADD81032.1), the candidate genes lie in the virion structural region, 22 and 21 annotated CDS after the putative LysA CDS, respectively. In ReqiDocB7, the possible candidate ADD80824.1 is 10 CDS after the putative LysA CDS. The source article also identifies LysA by sequence/domain evidence, without a protein-specific assay. Version-pinned coordinates and deposited feature labels are in the [candidate architecture table](../results/displaced_lysB_candidates/displaced_candidate_architectures.tsv).

These are useful experimental targets, not positive functional controls. The source study describes sequence-based candidates: it reports a relationship to mycobacteriophage LysB for Pepy6 and Poco6, and weak cutinase and LysB sequence similarity for DocB7 gp38. It reports whole-phage host-range or host-killing experiments, including reduced *R. equi* recovery after applying DocB7. It does not report purified-protein, gene-deletion or complementation evidence assigning those phenotypes to any candidate LysB. Accordingly, the candidates remain outside the experimentally supported LysB reference panel.

The mapping was regenerated against GenBank records GU580941.1, GU580942.1 and GU580940.1. A fresh NCBI Eutils retrieval reproduced the candidate table byte-for-byte with matching source checksums; see the [replay record](../results/displaced_lysB_candidates/replay_validation.json). Recreate the audit with `python scripts/audit_displaced_lysB_candidates.py --download`. The primary source is the [genomic and functional analysis of four *Rhodococcus* phages](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aem.01952-10).

Protein-specific activity and native-infection tests for these candidates would directly improve the evidence base for separated architectures. Until then, they do not make the primary conservation comparison estimable.
