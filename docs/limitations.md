# Limitations

The main limitation is functional coverage. The implemented independent search uses a small experimental reference panel concentrated in Ms6 and D29, with WXIN LysB adding one divergent biochemical example. Strongly diverged Gordonia proteins may fail the identity or coverage criteria even when they perform related functions. Their unresolved status is not evidence that the gene is absent. This limitation can remove the very displaced architectures the study aims to compare.

The confidence labels have strict operational meanings. High confidence requires an effectively exact reference match with full coverage. Moderate confidence uses at least 40% identity and 70% coverage on both sequences plus an E-value at most 1e-6. These criteria are transparent filters, not empirically calibrated probabilities of biochemical correctness. Family membership, a catalytic motif or an esterase annotation alone does not establish LysB specificity. The exploratory remote-LysB profiles produce candidates without establishing native function. Their limited held-out diversity and unresolved specificity prevent comprehensive detection claims for LysA, LysB or holins.

The reference sequences are also development controls. Reusing them as search targets cannot provide an independent estimate of sensitivity. The D29 capsid control and subsequent enzyme challenges provide limited specificity evidence; they do not represent the full diversity of non-lysis esterases or cell-wall enzymes. Published Yvonnetastic and DoobyDoo arrangements are predicted architecture controls; they do not independently validate the proteins' native biochemical roles. An unresolved control remains unresolved. Ms6 AF319619 is a lysis locus, and absence of an accessioned complete Ms6 genome in the audited metadata prevents treating it as a complete-genome cohort control.

The cohort is selected for completed, annotated, accessioned genomes from two host genera and for multiple available members per cluster. This favors lineages represented in public sequence projects. It is not a random ecological sample, and equal host allocation is not guaranteed. The pilot contains 20 genomes; mode targets of 50 and 300 are planning caps, not achieved sample sizes. The current selection loop considers two ranked candidates per lineage plus required controls. Any later change must be recorded before interpreting expanded samples.

GenBank completeness and annotations are not independent experimental ground truth. A complete-record description does not exclude an assembly error. Incorrect starts, missed small CDS, mistranslations, pseudogenes or split genes can alter distances and family assignments. Recalculated translation checks detect some inconsistencies but do not reannotate the genome. Partial and unresolved features are kept out of primary identification, which improves traceability at the cost of coverage. Alternative in-frame translation products such as Ms6 LysA are not reconstructed as independent genomic genes.

Distances depend on the submitted coordinate representation. Linear records do not wrap. Explicitly circular records use shortest genomic arcs, with ambiguous ties retained. A circularly permuted linear phage can have a reference origin that makes equivalent neighborhoods look truncated, and the conservative no-wrap policy can understate conservation near those ends. Exact deduplication is reverse-complement invariant but not invariant to arbitrary rotation. The bottom-1,000 canonical 21-mer Jaccard screen at 0.95 is a proxy, not ANI or exhaustive dereplication.

Sequence families depend on identity, coverage and MMseqs2 clustering behavior. A single family can merge divergent activities, while domain rearrangement or partial similarity can split related functions. Identity thresholds 0.30, 0.40 and 0.50 assess some sensitivity but do not establish a universal optimal threshold. Content-hashed family identifiers are reproducible for a fixed sequence membership; they can change when new distinct proteins enter the cohort. PhagesDB phams are not automatically equivalent to these study-specific families.

The comparison controls several measured properties but remains observational. It matches homologous anchors inside the same genome pair by local boundary completeness, position, protein length and family prevalence. It does not fully match expression, gene density, functional module, local rearrangement rate, family age or regulatory constraints. Neighboring controls may overlap each other or the target region. They contribute to a background mean rather than an independent sample size. Differences from that background cannot be read as selection or a special lysis mechanism.

Ancestry control is coarse. Genome-pair effects are averaged within PhagesDB clusters, and clusters receive equal weight, but mosaic phages can share ancestry across clusters. No validated whole-genome phylogeny, local gene tree or recombination history is reconstructed. Sign-flip inference requires independent clusters and sign-symmetric null contrasts. These assumptions are not guaranteed by matching. Five contributing clusters is a minimum reporting gate rather than proof of adequate power. Bootstrap intervals and leave-one-cluster-out analyses expose some instability but cannot solve identifiability.

Accordingly H1 and H4 remain descriptive, and H3 recurrent reorganization remains untestable in the present implementation. Similar separation in distinct clusters cannot reveal independent origin, direction, a rearrangement mechanism, selection or co-regulation. There is no transcriptomic or native interaction evidence for the newly analyzed proteins. The architecture candidate ranking prioritizes reviewable observations, not experimentally verified mechanisms or novel biology.

Database classifications and accession annotations can change over time. The cohort manifest fixes the accession versions used here; raw metadata exports are retrieved separately. Frozen versions and checksums support reproducibility, while continued availability depends on the source services.

The original panel supports an A/B pair only in D29. The combined reference panel improves supported LysA coverage to 10/20 genomes, while supported LysB remains 1/20 and no eligible H2 lineage contrast exists. The remote-profile candidates do not change those primary calls. The displaced control retains annotation-level separation without independent functional recovery. Expansion is gated until functional identification and lineage replication support the planned comparison. See [reference-expansion results](amendment_results.md) and [validation](validation.md).

The subsequent 39-reference assay follow-up raises supported B coverage to 3/20 by identifying two exact occurrences of the assayed Bongo protein sequence. These occurrences are not independent biochemical validation families. Both genomes lack independently supported A assignments, leaving their architectures unresolved and the primary conservation comparison untestable. This improves sequence coverage while retaining the limits of the original evolutionary question. [Assay-reference results](lysterase_update_results.md).

The annotation sensitivity permits four descriptive comparisons, including two with separated annotations. Deposited labels may incorporate context, so these conditional scores cannot independently validate the primary functional hypothesis. Their small number, overlapping background windows and dependence on operational family/window settings limit interpretation. The primary high/moderate catalogue and its unavailable H2 estimate remain separate. [Annotation sensitivity](annotation_neighborhood_results.md).

The complete assay-source survey adds five linked A/B observations but no supported separated control. Its thirteen genomes were selected because they supplied assayed B sequences; B self-recovery is expected and does not estimate independent sensitivity. Eight sources still lack supported A assignments. These results identify an A-coverage limitation within this control collection, not a population frequency of missing or linked lysis genes. [Source architecture survey](reference_architecture_results.md).

A targeted literature follow-up maps Giles, PDRPxv, BTCU-1 and RitSun assay sources to four additional linked gene neighborhoods. Three full articles report functional assays for both enzyme classes; a conference abstract reports both for RitSun without resolving the tested LysA accession. PDRPxv's reported LysB amplicon length differs by one nucleotide from its deposited CDS, and its recombinant insert was not sequenced. These added studies provide no experimentally supported separated arrangement. They do not alter the 20-genome calls or make H2 estimable. [Assayed allele mapping](assayed_reference_allele_results.md).

Three *Rhodococcus* phage genomes provide sequence-mapped, literature-proposed displaced LysB candidates. Although one source study reports whole-phage host killing, none of the candidate proteins has a reported protein-specific assay, deletion phenotype or rescue experiment there. The candidates are experimental priorities rather than functional controls, and do not change the primary eligibility result. [Displaced candidate audit](displaced_lysB_candidate_audit.md).
